The treatment

What it is

Botulinum toxin type A is a prescription medicine. Injected in very small doses into specific facial muscles, it relaxes them for a few months. Lines that form when those muscles contract, such as the furrows between the brows when you frown, soften because the skin is no longer folded as hard or as often.

It works on dynamic lines, the ones caused by movement. Lines that are etched in even when your face is still, or that come from lost volume or sagging skin, usually need a different treatment or a combination. That is decided at the consultation.

"Botox" is the brand name most people use for the whole category. Several licensed products exist, and the one used for you is named in your written plan.

Areas

What it is used for

Expression lines

  • Forehead lines: horizontal lines from raising the brows.
  • Frown lines: the vertical "11" lines between the brows.
  • Crow's feet: lines at the outer corners of the eyes when you smile.
  • Bunny lines: small lines on the sides of the nose when you scrunch it.

Shaping and other uses

  • Brow lift: a small lift at the tail of the brow.
  • Gummy smile: less gum showing when you smile.
  • Jaw slimming: relaxing an enlarged masseter (chewing) muscle to soften a square jawline.
  • Underarm sweating: reducing excessive sweating (hyperhidrosis).

Not every area suits every face. Dr. Bindiya will tell you plainly if a treatment won't give you the result you're hoping for.

1. Consultation

Your face is examined at rest and in movement, and your medical history is taken. You'll be told which areas are worth treating, which aren't, and what result is realistic.

2. A written plan and cost

The areas, the product, the approximate dose and the cost are written down for you before anything is done. The cost depends on the areas and the dose, so it is quoted after the assessment rather than as a flat price.

3. The injections

The skin is cleaned and numbing cream can be applied if you'd like. A very fine needle is used for a few small injections per area. Most people describe a brief pinch. You can go back to work the same day.

4. Review

The effect builds over about two weeks. A review around then lets the doctor check the result and make small adjustments if needed.

Safety

Side effects, suitability and aftercare

Common, short-lived effects

Small bumps, redness or tenderness where the needle went in, which usually settle within an hour or so. Some people get a light bruise or a mild headache for a day or two.

Less common

Occasionally the product affects a neighbouring muscle and causes a temporary droop of an eyelid or brow, or an uneven look. These effects wear off as the treatment does. Careful dosing and placement keep the risk low, which is one reason the injections are given by the doctor.

When it may not be suitable

Treatment is usually postponed or avoided during pregnancy and breastfeeding, with an infection at the injection site, with certain nerve and muscle conditions, or with a known allergy to the product. Some medicines also matter, so bring a list of what you take. The doctor makes the final decision on suitability in person.

Aftercare

You'll be given written instructions. Typical advice is to avoid rubbing or massaging the area and to skip strenuous exercise for the rest of the day.

Questions

Common questions

Does it hurt?

The needle is very fine and most people describe a brief pinch at each point. Numbing cream can be applied first. The whole appointment takes about 15 to 20 minutes.

Will my face look frozen?

A frozen look usually comes from a standard dose used without reading the individual face. Here the dose is planned around your muscle strength and how you move your face, aiming for softer lines with your expressions intact.

How long does it last?

Typically three to six months. It varies with metabolism, muscle strength, dose and how long you've been having treatment.

When should I have it before a wedding or event?

Three to four weeks before, so the effect has fully settled and there's time for a review.

Do men have this treatment?

Yes. Forehead lines, frown lines and jaw slimming are commonly treated in men. Doses are often different because the muscles are usually stronger.

How much does it cost?

It depends on the areas treated and the dose, so a fair price can only be given after the assessment. You get the cost in writing before anything is done.
